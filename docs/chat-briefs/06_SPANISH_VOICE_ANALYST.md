# Chat Brief 06 — Spanish Voice Analyst

**Role:** Owner of the Spanish-language AI voice interview  
**Required context:** Master Brief, English interview data contract, paid questionnaire schema, Legal recording requirements

## Mission

Design a natural Spanish AI business analyst for Spanish-speaking business owners. The Spanish experience must be culturally and linguistically adapted while producing the same canonical data as the English line.

## Responsibilities

- Write and maintain the Spanish system prompt
- Adapt rather than literally translate the interview
- Define opening, recording disclosure, consent confirmation, and closing
- Build Spanish universal, industry, and process modules
- Handle English product names and bilingual terminology naturally
- Handle limited code-switching without losing the selected language path
- Define clarification and recovery language
- Map every response to the canonical shared schema
- Create Spanish-native QA criteria and test dialogues

## Language principles

- Use clear, neutral business Spanish suitable for a broad U.S. audience
- Avoid unnecessary regional slang
- Prefer commonly understood business terminology
- Explain technical terms briefly when needed
- Keep product and software names in their recognized form
- Preserve the customer’s original wording in the transcript
- Do not switch the entire interview to English merely because the user names English software

## Structural parity requirement

The Spanish line must collect equivalent evidence to the English line. Differences in phrasing are allowed; missing data fields or different business logic are not.

## Must not do

- Do not translate word-for-word when it sounds unnatural
- Do not change scoring, product scope, or report promises
- Do not create a unique Spanish output schema
- Do not mix marketing sales pressure into the diagnostic interview
- Do not claim financial outcomes

## Required deliverables

1. Spanish system prompt
2. Spanish conversation state machine
3. Localized universal and modular question library
4. Terminology glossary
5. Code-switching handling rules
6. Canonical output mapping
7. Spanish QA rubric
8. At least 15 Spanish test dialogues

## Acceptance criteria

- Native or near-native Spanish reviewers consider the dialogue natural
- English and Spanish interviews produce schema-compatible outputs
- Recording consent is clear
- The bot handles software names and mixed terminology correctly
- The Spanish path is independently testable through its own phone number
